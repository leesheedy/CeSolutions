## 1. What the check found

**The site never states a benefit above the fold.** The H1 is "Powering a Sustainable Future" and the first paragraph is about the company, not the customer. The only savings figure on the whole homepage ("50% to 100%") is buried in the sixth FAQ accordion.

**Proof exists but is hidden.** CES is a NETCC Approved Seller with a 4.8/5 rating from 26 SolarQuotes reviews, in-house CEC-accredited electricians, Tesla/BYD/Sungrow/SigEnergy/Fronius/Enphase products, and 15+ years of Daniel's experience. None of that appears on the homepage.

**No rebate messaging.** The federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program (about 30% off a battery, stepping down every six months) and the Solar Victoria $1,400 panel rebate are the two biggest reasons to act now. Neither is mentioned.

**Smaller defects to fix while you're in there:**
- "Solar Made Simple" card: "from design, from installation to aftercare" (duplicate "from").
- FAQ page, EV answer starts "es. Solar can be used" (missing Y).
- "Why Locals Choose CES" nav item links to `/clean-energy-solutions-book-online/`, and the batteries page lives at `/heat-pumps/benefits-of-batteries/`. Both slugs mislead Google and visitors.
- Batteries page shows "times more energy 0" three times (a broken counter widget).
- Homepage About copy says "over three decades of collective experience" in the hero and "more than 30 years combined" below it. Pick one figure and use it once.
- Meta description is about the company ("family-owned and passionate"), not about what the visitor gets.

## 2. Market research: how competitors word savings

| Company | Headline savings claim | Proof next to it |
|---|---|---|
| Stag Electrical (Albury-Wodonga) | "$1,000 to $2,200+ per year", payback "3 to 6 years" | 18 yrs, 10,000+ installs, 4.8 Google (100+), 25-yr workmanship guarantee |
| BVR Energy (Wodonga) | "Cut your electricity bills by up to 50%" | 25 yrs, 15,000 panels installed, "$3 million saved annually" |
| KDEC (Albury-Wodonga) | Customer stories: "$400+ quarterly bill down to $70" | 32 yrs, 18,689 projects, NETCC signatory |
| Zip Solar (national) | "Slash your power bills by up to 80%", business "up to 70%" | 150+ 5-star reviews, NETCC, 25-yr panel warranty |
| CES today | "50% to 100%" (FAQ only) | none on homepage |

**Three patterns worth copying:**
1. **Dollars beat percentages.** Stag and KDEC lead with dollar figures or real bill examples. A percentage needs the reader to do maths on a bill they don't remember. "$1,500 a year" lands instantly.
2. **The claim never stands alone.** Every strong competitor puts the payback period and a trust number (rating, years, installs) in the same viewport as the savings claim.
3. **Local specificity.** Stag's subhead is "Built for Border Climate Conditions." Naming Albury-Wodonga in the H1 is both an SEO and a trust move.

**Industry benchmarks you can cite** (2026, 6.6 kW residential system): solar only saves $1,200–$2,000 a year; a battery adds $800–$1,500 on top; typical payback 3–6 years. Sources: Stag Electrical, Set Solar, Green.com.au, Ecogeneration (all 2026 articles).

## 3. Compliance: can you say "Save up to X%"?

Yes, with three conditions.

1. **Australian Consumer Law (s18, s29).** An "up to" claim is judged by the overall impression on a reasonable consumer. If only a handful of customers ever reach the top figure, the ACCC treats the claim as misleading. "Up to 100%" is the risky one: it implies a zero bill, which is only true for households with a large battery and modest evening use. Drop it.
2. **New Energy Tech Consumer Code.** CES is a NETCC Approved Seller. The Code (re-authorised by the ACCC on 28 August 2026 for five years) requires that "any claims relating to performance and energy cost savings are reasonably based and, where available, based on reputable sources." Keep a one-page substantiation note on file: which figures you used, where they came from, and the assumptions (system size, self-consumption rate, tariff).
3. **Qualify it in the same viewport.** A short line under the headline ("Typical 6.6 kW system, based on 2026 Victorian tariffs. Your figure depends on usage and system size.") is enough. Don't hide it in a footer.

**Recommendation:** lead with "up to 80%", support it with the dollar range and payback, and replace the 80% with your own installed-customer average as soon as you have it (your monitoring data can give you this). A number that says "our customers" beats any industry figure.

### FAQ (homepage, six questions)

**How much does a solar system cost in Albury-Wodonga?**
A typical 6.6 kW home system with quality panels and inverter runs from around $5,000 to $8,000 after the federal STC discount. Batteries add $8,000 to $15,000 before the federal battery rebate. We quote from your bills, so you get a real number, not a range.

**How much will I save?**
Most households cut their bill by 50% to 80% with panels alone. Adding a battery usually pushes that higher because you use your own stored power in the evening instead of buying it back at peak rates. We'll estimate your figure from your actual usage before you commit.

**How long until it pays for itself?**
Usually 3 to 6 years for panels, longer for a battery. After that, the power is free for the remaining 20-plus years of the system's life.

**What rebates can I get?**
The federal STC discount on panels (everyone), the federal Cheaper Home Batteries discount (everyone, stepping down every six months), and the Solar Victoria panel rebate of up to $1,400 (Victorian owner-occupiers under the income cap). We apply for all of them on your behalf.

**Does solar work on cloudy days?**
Yes, at reduced output. Panels don't generate at night, which is where a battery comes in: it stores your daytime surplus so you can run the house on it after dark.

**Will solar add value to my home?**
Buyers see a lower running cost, and most agents treat a quality system as a plus. The exact uplift varies, so we don't quote a figure.

## 6. Substantiation note to keep on file

Keep this with your NETCC documentation.

- "Up to 80%": based on a 6.6 kW system offsetting 70 to 85% of consumption for households with daytime usage or a battery; consistent with published 2026 industry figures (Zip Solar, Isolux, Green.com.au) and CES's own FAQ range.
- "$1,200 to $3,000 a year": 2026 industry range for 6.6 kW solar-only ($1,200–$2,000) plus battery uplift ($800–$1,500). Sources: Set Solar, Stag Electrical, Ecogeneration, 2026.
- "3 to 6 years payback": consistent with Stag Electrical, CES FAQ and SolarQuotes guidance.
- "Around 30% off a battery": Cheaper Home Batteries Program, DCCEEW, launched 1 July 2025; rate reduced 1 May 2026 and steps down six-monthly.
- "Up to $1,400": Solar Victoria panel rebate, income threshold reduced to $150,000 from 1 July 2026.

Replace the industry figures with your own installed-customer averages when you have 12 months of monitoring data from 20 or more systems.
